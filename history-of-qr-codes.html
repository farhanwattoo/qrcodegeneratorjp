History of QR codes

QR codes started as an industrial tracking tool and later became a mainstream bridge between the physical world and the mobile web. Their rise came from one simple strength: they encode more information and scan faster than traditional barcodes.

Where QR codes began

QR stands for "quick response". The format was developed in the mid-1990s for manufacturing and supply-chain use, where teams needed faster scanning and more data capacity than one-dimensional barcodes could provide.

The original problem was not marketing. It was operational efficiency: tracking parts accurately, quickly, and at multiple angles.

Why the format spread

  • It can hold far more data than a classic barcode.
  • Finder patterns make the code easier to detect and orient quickly.
  • Error correction lets scanners recover even if part of the code is damaged.
  • Smartphones turned almost every pocket into a QR reader.

Major phases of adoption

  • Industrial use for parts, logistics, and manufacturing workflows
  • Early consumer experiments in print advertising and packaging
  • Wider mobile adoption as phone cameras and operating systems improved
  • Rapid mainstream use for payments, menus, event access, and onboarding
  • Modern growth in product experiences, apps, identity, and automation

Why QR codes matter today

Modern QR codes are no longer just a shortcut to a URL. They now support payments, dynamic campaign routing, access control, tickets, device setup, menus, documents, and internal workflows across retail, hospitality, healthcare, and software products.
